# run-601: The Dependency, Blocker Triage & Outcome Scoring Lab

**Kind:** interaction-lab  
**Port:** 3601  
**DB:** none  
**Question:** Are a blocker, a cross-project dependency, and a calendar clash best served by a single unified interaction model in the UI (§2.3 Q1), and which interaction mechanic—an Ego-Graph Drawer, a Stoplight Triage Table, or a Cascading Pushback Timeline—most effectively surfaces 4-step blocker severity and 4-step item outcome scoring (§2.3 Q2, Q3) without drowning the user in a tangled spaghetti graph?

## Scenario

A team of developers with a C/C++ systems background is designing the interaction architecture for Lerch's dependency, blocker, and scoring systems. In complex software projects, work rarely moves in isolation: leaf tasks run into missing tools, projects depend on shared infrastructure, and schedules collide. In Lerch, blockers surface issues across items—including across distinct projects (e.g., an item *"cut the bread"* under *"dinner"* blocked by *"bread knife"* under *"buy cutlery"*, §2.3)—and passively notify the team.

Traditional project management tools often force a false dichotomy: either flat, uninformative blocker flags that hide causal chains, or massive global node-link dependency graphs (DAGs) that devolve into illegible "spaghetti graphs" as soon as a workspace scales past a dozen tasks. Furthermore, teams struggle with where impediments and outcomes belong: should a blocker live as an informal tag on a card, or as a formal directed relation? Where does an item's outcome score live before and after evaluation?

The developer opens the interaction lab at `http://localhost:3601` (`DB=none`, using fast, reactive in-memory fixtures) to evaluate three distinct interaction mechanics for exploring and resolving impediments:

1. **Ego-Graph Drawer:** A slide-over panel focused strictly on the selected item's immediate 1-hop horizon: direct blockers (inbound) and direct dependents (outbound). Clicking any connected node re-centers the ego-graph on that node with a smooth transition, allowing the user to trace causal chains step by step without rendering an overwhelming global web.
2. **Stoplight Triage Table:** A dense, high-contrast operational dashboard where blocked items bubble to the top sorted by the 4-step **Blocker severity** scale (`I cannot do anything` → `I will be stuck` → `I can proceed, but slowed down` → `just nice to have`). Clicking any row expands inline blocker details, affected items, and triage actions (e.g., adjusting severity, reassigning, or marking resolved).
3. **Cascading Pushback Timeline:** A horizontal chronological view mapping scheduled items and allocations. When a user drags a blocker item to extend its duration or delay its start, dependent items visually shift downstream with animated ripple vectors and immediate visual clash warnings (§2.4).

Across these views, the developer tests the relationship between item cards, the triage dashboard, and Definition of Ready (DoR) gates:
- **Card vs. Dashboard Representation (§2.3 Q2):** Evaluating what minimal blocker cues belong directly on a kanban card or list row (e.g., compact stoplight pill, unread blocker pulse, DoR lock icon) versus what details belong strictly in the slide-over drawer or triage table.
- **Pre-Triage vs. Post-Triage Lifecycle (§2.3 Q3):** Testing how a blocker begins as a simple on-item severity tag (accessible during quick capture) and seamlessly promotes to a first-class directed dependency record linking `blocker_item_id` and `blocked_item_id`.
- **Outcome Scoring vs. Blocker Severity (§2.3):** Contrasting the 4-step **Item score** (`doesn't work` → `bare minimum` → `satisfactory` → `excellent`) with the 4-step **Blocker severity** to ensure quality evaluations and progress blockers remain visually and conceptually distinct.
- **Unified vs. Specialized Impedance Models (§2.3 Q1):** Toggling between a *Unified Model* (where blockers, cross-project dependencies, and calendar clashes share the same visual language, severity scale, and DoR gates) and a *Specialized Model* (where each has bespoke UI widgets and separate handling).

Full database persistence, user authentication, WebSocket server infrastructure, and binary file uploads are intentionally omitted to keep the prototype strictly focused on client-side interaction ergonomics, spatial clarity, and cognitive load.

## What to Evaluate

### 1. Visual / Presentation Aspects
- **Ego-Graph vs. Spaghetti Graph:** Visual clarity of the 1-hop ego-graph drawer with directional arrows and 1-click re-centering versus full DAG visualizers. Evaluating whether restricting the visual field to immediate antecedents and descendants eliminates cognitive overload.
- **Stoplight Severity Spectrum:** High-contrast visual hierarchy of the 4-step blocker severity scale (`cannot do anything` in critical red, `will be stuck` in warning orange, `slowed down` in caution yellow, `nice to have` in muted neutral). Assessing readability in both dense tabular views and compact kanban card chips.
- **Outcome Score Representation:** Visual styling of the 4-step item outcome score (`doesn't work` → `excellent`) on completed items and review cards. Ensuring developers never confuse outcome quality with blocker severity or priority.
- **Card-Level Footprint (§2.3 Q2):** Visual density and restraint on kanban cards. Determining whether a single stoplight icon with a hover tooltip is sufficient, or if card faces need explicit blocker titles and DoR badge counters.
- **Pushback & Clash Indicators (§2.4):** Visual feedback on the timeline when shifting work: ripple animations on dependent items, hatched highlight zones for calendar clashes, and clear lead-lag connection lines.

### 2. Procedural / Workflow Aspects
- **Triage & Promotion Ergonomics (§2.3 Q3):** The procedural flow of flagging an impediment: starting as a quick single-click severity tag on an item, and subsequently linking it to a concrete blocking item or external project dependency in the triage table.
- **Ego-Graph Traversal Speed:** Keystroke and click efficiency when tracing multi-hop dependency chains (e.g., A blocks B blocks C) via 1-click re-centering in the ego drawer compared to searching and filtering lists.
- **Definition of Ready Enforcement:** How the UI communicates that an item cannot start because upstream blockers are unresolved. Evaluating soft visual prompts (warning banners on drag, DoR checklist items) versus hard blocked states.
- **Unified Impedance Workflow (§2.3 Q1):** Operational friction when triaging a task blocker, a cross-project dependency, and a calendar scheduling clash side by side. Testing whether treating all three as variants of "work that impedes work" simplifies the user's mental model.
- **Simulated Real-Time Escalation (W2/W6 triggers):** How the UI responds when a blocker severity is escalated or resolved asynchronously (simulated event feed), testing toast alerts, badge color transitions, and table re-sorting.

### 3. Technology / Stack Choices (Libraries, DX, Ergonomics, Suitability)
- **Graph & Connector Rendering:** Assessing whether bespoke Vue 3 SVG components (computed Bézier paths and CSS transitions) provide better developer ergonomics, bundle size (<20 KB), and reactivity than heavyweight graph libraries (e.g., Vue Flow, D3, Cytoscape) for 1-hop ego-graphs.
- **Timeline & Drag Interaction:** Evaluating pointer-event drag mechanics and CSS transforms for the cascading pushback timeline in Vue 3, measuring responsiveness and frame stability when a drag updates multiple dependent nodes.
- **In-Memory Graph State & Cycle Detection:** Designing a reactive in-memory dependency store in Pinia or Vue `reactive`: assessing algorithm efficiency (topological sort, Tarjan's cycle detection) to immediately prevent circular dependencies (A blocks B blocks A) with instant user feedback.
- **Component Composability:** Evaluating how cleanly the Stoplight Triage Table, Ego Drawer, and DoR badges share a unified underlying dependency data structure without duplicating reactive state.

## Done When

- [ ] In-memory fixtures seed cleanly with realistic multi-project work trees, including cross-project dependencies (e.g., *"cut the bread"* blocked by *"bread knife"*), internal task blockers, and calendar time clashes.
- [ ] Item cards and detail views render both 4-step scales with distinct, non-conflicting visual styles: **Blocker severity** (`cannot do anything`, `will be stuck`, `slowed down`, `nice to have`) and **Item score** (`doesn't work`, `bare minimum`, `satisfactory`, `excellent`).
- [ ] Ego-Graph Drawer opens upon selecting any item, displaying its immediate 1-hop inbound blockers and outbound dependents with smooth directional connectors and 1-click node re-centering.
- [ ] Stoplight Triage Table displays all blocked items sorted by severity, with expandable rows showing root-cause blockers, affected items, and inline triage actions (severity adjustment, dependency linking, resolution).
- [ ] Cascading Pushback Timeline allows interactive dragging of a scheduled blocker item, visually propagating delays to dependent items with animated ripple indicators and calendar clash highlights.
- [ ] Interactive toggle allows switching between a *Unified Impedance Model* (blockers, cross-project dependencies, and calendar clashes in a single visual language) and a *Specialized Model* (separate widgets for each) to compare cognitive clarity.
- [ ] Blocked items display a Definition of Ready (DoR) indicator listing blocking dependencies, with visual feedback preventing or warning against moving the item to `started`.
- [ ] Cycle detection logic rejects circular dependency creation (e.g., A blocks B blocks A) with clear, immediate validation feedback.
- [ ] Simulated event trigger simulates an incoming blocker escalation or resolution event (prepping `W2`/`W6`), demonstrating reactive badge updates and automated triage table re-sorting.
- [ ] Scoped documentation (`docs/run-601/design.md`, `frontend.md`, `backend.md`, `NOTES.md`) can be scaffolded and maintained cleanly from this brief.
